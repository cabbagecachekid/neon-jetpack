neon-jetpack

Your resume, still yours.

A free pack of Claude skills for people writing resumes, cover letters, and portfolios with AI. It polishes your drafts, flags the phrases that read as machine-written, and refuses to quietly inflate a single claim.

Why this exists

AI editing has a heavy hand.

This pack came out of a real revision session. While refining a resume and portfolio with AI, small edits kept creeping in: a claim about independent work drifted toward something that sounded like founding a program, and plain headers turned into generic filler that could have belonged to anyone.

Nobody approved those changes. They just accumulated.

neon-jetpack assumes you are doing the healthy thing, collaborating with Claude on your own writing. Its job is to make sure the result still says what you meant, claims only what is true, and sounds like you on your best day rather than a template on its average one.

Agentic workflows

Say one sentence. The team runs the chain.

These are not one-shot prompts. A dispatcher skill routes your request to the right specialists, in the right order, with a sign-off gate before anything changes.

Ship a cover letter

A letter that reads like you on a good day, checked for inflation before it reaches a hiring manager.

  1. line-edit cleans grammar, wordiness, and awkward sentences
  2. full-review runs six passes: AI tells, tone, honesty, register, facts
  3. You sign off on a diff table before anything changes

Why this order: line-edit is a light polish that never touches tone, so it goes first on a rough draft. Full-review already includes the AI-tells and tone checks, so you never run those separately. One clean pass, one deep pass, done.

Say this to Claude

Line-edit this cover letter draft, then run a full review before I send it to a hiring manager.

Portfolio page going live

A page reviewed for structure first and words second, so your copy edits survive.

  1. ux-web-design-review audits hierarchy, readability, and trust, with every claim cited
  2. You fix the structural issues it finds
  3. full-review then works over the final copy in place

Why this order: if the UX pass tells you to cut a section or reorder the page, any copy edits you made earlier are wasted. Structure first, words second. The reverse order costs you a rewrite.

Say this to Claude

This portfolio page is about to go live. Run a UX review of the page first, then a full review of the copy once the structure is settled.

The quick “does this sound like AI?” pass

Every mechanical tell flagged, each with a concrete rewrite. Five minutes, no ceremony.

  1. ai-written-check scans for em dashes, triple lists, engineered cadence, and the rest
  2. That is it. One skill, one pass.

There is no chain here, and that is the point. A focused check is faster than the full treatment. But know the limit: this pass catches how the writing sounds, not whether it overclaims. When the reader is a hiring manager, ask for a full review instead.

Say this to Claude

Run an AI-written check on this LinkedIn post. Just the tells, nothing deeper.

Release your creative work

Finished copy plus a plain-English walkthrough of US copyright basics before you hit publish.

  1. line-edit or full-review, whichever your draft needs
  2. You sign off on the final text
  3. copyright-creative-work covers registration, notice, and licensing questions

Why this order: the copyright pass is educational and assumes the work is final. Run it after the copy is locked, so the answers apply to what you actually publish. It is a primer, not legal advice, and the skill says so up front.

Say this to Claude

Review this artist statement for my photo series, then walk me through the copyright basics before I publish it.

The crew

Seven specialists and a dispatcher.

line-edit

Light-touch sentence polish: grammar, clarity, wordiness. Hands back a clean version plus the judgment calls you can overrule. Never a rewrite.

“Polish this email.”

ai-written-check

Flags the mechanical tells of machine writing, from em dashes to engineered cadence, each with a concrete rewrite.

“Does this sound like AI?”

cringe-check

Audits tone and positioning: solo-hero framing, parroting the job description, overclaiming authority.

“Does this sound cocky?”

full-review

The orchestrator for high-stakes copy. Six passes, a before/after diff table, and nothing changes until you sign off.

“Review my cover letter.”

ux-web-design-review

Reviews your portfolio page against cited UX research, separating what works for readers from what is merely in fashion.

“Review my site’s UX.”

journey-map

Turns interviews and funnel data into a milestone-based journey map: milestones, not screens, with every claim source-tagged or loudly marked ASSUMED. Builds in FigJam when Figma is connected, or as a portable markdown matrix.

“Map the journey from signup to renewal.”

copyright-creative-work

US copyright basics for your own creative work: registration prep, split sheets, what to do before you publish. Educational, not legal advice.

“How do I protect this before I post it?”

The contract

Four rules every skill obeys.

  • Sign-off firstFindings are presented before anything is rewritten. Your words change only when you approve the change.
  • Never inflateIf an edit would make a claim bigger than the stated truth, the skill stops and asks instead of polishing the inflation into a fact.
  • Preserve voiceA rough, specific sentence beats a smooth, generic one. These skills subtract noise; they do not add polish-paste.
  • Report the gapsEvery review ends by naming which passes ran and which did not, so “reviewed” never overstates coverage.

Free, MIT-licensed

Install in one command.

In Claude Code, add the marketplace and install the pack:

/plugin marketplace add cabbagecachekid/neon-jetpack
/plugin install neon-jetpack@neon-jetpack

Prefer just one skill? Each folder under skills/ stands alone. Copy it into ~/.claude/skills/, or upload it as a skill on claude.ai. full-review falls back to built-in notes if the focused checks are not installed.
